Affect & emotion in learning with AI

AI in education & human–AI collaboration in learning

How AI reshapes the way students write, inquire, and make sense of ideas together, in classrooms and in the online spaces where learning now happens.

03

Ethical AI in education & learning-technology design

Building AI-enabled learning tools and asking what they do to students' autonomy, ownership, and sense of fairness.

AbsorbIt!

Tangible interactive systemBeyond Bits & Atoms (Dr. Paulo Blikstein)with Ran JuConference submission in progress

▶ Watch the demo on YouTube (opens in a new tab)

AbsorbIt! was developed as the final project for Dr. Paulo Blikstein's Beyond Bits and Atoms course at Teachers College, Columbia University, designed and built with Ran Ju. A conference submission is in progress.

Read the paper (PDF) Watch the demo

K-12 nutrition education has long focused on behavior, helping students make healthy food choices, rather than on the physiological mechanisms behind those choices. As a result, nutritional synergy, the way nutrients interact to enhance or inhibit each other's absorption, goes largely unaddressed. AbsorbIt! is a tangible interactive learning system for children ages 6 to 10 in a museum setting, designed to make these invisible physiological processes explorable through hands-on experimentation.

How it works

The experience runs in four stages, structured as an inquiry cycle. First, a short narrated animation introduces basic nutrition and ends on a synergy problem: a carrot character explains that it carries Vitamin A, but that Vitamin A cannot be absorbed without help from another food. This frames a concrete question for children to investigate.

Children then drop colored balls, each representing a food, into a life-size digestive-system poster. A webcam fitted to the poster feeds a Teachable Machine model that recognizes which foods were chosen, and a real-time p5.js particle simulation at the small intestine shows how that specific combination affects absorption. Children can reset and try new pairings, dropping carrots alone, then adding olive oil, then swapping it for lemon, and watch the simulation respond differently each time.

What children learn

The learning goal is conceptual: nutrients interact during absorption. Vitamin A needs fat to cross the intestinal wall, and plant-based iron is absorbed far better in the presence of Vitamin C. Rather than trying to change eating behavior directly, AbsorbIt! builds understanding of nutrient interaction as an investigable biological phenomenon.

Why this design

The system draws on inquiry-based learning and on research into tangible interaction, which is most valuable when the physical action itself carries conceptual meaning. Most science simulations are screen-only, with no physical input that mirrors the phenomenon. AbsorbIt! couples physical ball input with real-time simulation output, so a child's food choice and its biological consequence are immediately and visibly connected, adding kinetic, sensorimotor detail that screen interaction alone cannot.

pH Detector AR

Augmented realityMachine-learning color classificationTransformative Learning Technologies Lab

Overview

pH Detector AR is an augmented-reality chemistry tool that reads the color of a pH test strip through a phone camera and returns a live pH estimate, so students can run hands-on acid–base experiments without dedicated lab equipment.

How it works

A machine-learning color classifier maps the strip's color to a point on the pH scale, with automated white-balance calibration so readings stay stable under different lighting. The interface overlays a 1–14 scale on the camera view, highlights where the sample falls, and names the result in plain terms (for example, weak base for a reading around pH 9–10), turning an invisible chemical property into something students can see and investigate in real time.

Built in the Transformative Learning Technologies Lab (Dr. Paulo Blikstein), Teachers College, Columbia University.

Navigating Guilt and Efficiency: How IB Students in South Korea Negotiate Authorship and Affect with Generative AI

Oh, D. (2026)Poster presented at ICOLSEI 2026Sole-authored
Read the paper (PDF)

Abstract

This exploratory study examines how final-year International Baccalaureate (IB) students in South Korea negotiate affect and authorship when using generative AI in high-stakes writing. Through semi-structured interviews analyzed with reflexive thematic analysis, it traces a five-stage affective trajectory across the writing process, from ideation to submission.

Students' use was mediated by what I call an academic survival override: a prioritizing of competitive viability over authorship concerns, under which initial discomfort gave way, through repeated use, to affective desensitization. The study surfaces the emotional and ethical tensions students carry when AI becomes part of how they write.

Where it's going

I am now extending this work to global online discourse (the r/IBO community) under an IRB-exempt protocol in a doctoral-level digital research methods course, to see whether the patterns found among Korean second-language writers hold across the broader international IB population.

Everyone Had Something to Add: Peer Knowledge Building and Its Limits during Student Data Collection in High School Science

Bae, H., Xu, X., Oh, D., Qin, Y., Shim, J., & Yoon, S.In preparation for ISLS

Full paper available on request.

Overview

This study looks at how students interact during the data-collection phase of project-based science lessons built on real-world air-quality data. Using interaction analysis, we examine how peer-to-peer moves, affirmation, confirmation, and shared surprise, let students build on one another's ideas and contribute to community knowledge as epistemic agents.

It also asks where that process reaches its limits: how a heavy reliance on affirmation, rather than critical challenge, can quietly constrain genuine inquiry even as it keeps collaboration going.

My contribution centers on the data-driven inquiry and peer-interaction strand of the framework and findings.

"What if Jurassic Park was Real?": Distributed Epistemic Repair Around AI-Generated Science Media

Sotério, C., Xu, X., Bae, H., Oh, D., Qin, Y., & Blikstein, P.In preparation

Full paper available on request.

Abstract

Generative AI can produce convincing scientific representations without ensuring their accuracy, raising questions about how scientific inquiry develops socially around unreliable media. We examined 220 selected TikTok and Reddit comments using scientific-practice coding and reply-sequence analysis. Replies revealed how participants developed explanations, challenged claims, and introduced corrections following questions, disagreements, and humor.

Argumentation was more frequent on Reddit (44% vs. 28%), whereas questioning was more frequent on TikTok (27% vs. 10%). Explanations and arguments were also more frequent in replies (72%) than in new-thread comments (38%). We conceptualize these interactions as a form of distributed epistemic repair, through which participants negotiate scientific coherence around unstable representations.

Our findings illustrate how scientific reasoning can become interactionally organized without intentionally coordinated collaboration, extending accounts of distributed sensemaking to transient, informal participation around AI-generated media.

Lazy or Innovative? The Public Debate Over AI-Generated Brand Advertising

Oh, D. (2026)Course paper, MSTU 5512 (AI & Society)Highest grade in class
Read the paper (PDF)

Overview

A case study of the public backlash to Valentino's December 2025 DeVain campaign, which was clearly labeled as AI-generated yet still drew immediate criticism across the BBC and trade press within forty-eight hours. The study reads the debate through three keywords, hype, labor, and art, to ask how the public negotiates the boundaries of acceptable AI use in luxury advertising.

Using reflexive thematic analysis of highly engaged Instagram comments, it shows how audiences treat luxury as an implicit contract of human craftsmanship, the labor illusion in which visible effort creates value, and experience generative AI as a violation of that contract. The debate reveals cultural definitions of authenticity and labor at a moment when people want human presence more than ever.
